= ADR-033: Derived evidence is labelled, and absence carries a cause

== Status

Accepted.

== Context

Joining the service's own telemetry to the load generator's view of each stage is what makes "what
changed as load increased" answerable. Before it, a run produced one window and one peak per metric —
enough to say the connection pool reached 98%, and not enough to say when.

Doing the join raised two questions that are easy to answer badly.

The first is *how Vortex knows when each stage was in effect*. The obvious method is a cumulative walk
over the planned stage durations. It is usually right and occasionally not: a load generator that
starts slowly, a stage that overruns, a machine that stalls, and the computed boundary drifts from the
real one — while looking exactly as authoritative as a measured one.

The second is *what to say when a provider does not answer*. Vortex already reported missing telemetry
rather than defaulting it to zero, which is the more important half of being honest. But a report that
says only "JVM memory telemetry unavailable" gives an engineer nothing to act on. Whether the query
matched no series, the token was refused, or the host was unreachable are three different problems and
three different afternoons.

== Decision

=== Stage alignment carries its basis

`StageWindowBasis` is either `OBSERVED` or `DERIVED_FROM_PLAN`, and it travels on `StageTelemetry` and
on `StageObservation` into every report and export.

`OBSERVED` is reachable without touching the generated script. k6 already emits `vus` samples, which
the aggregator previously discarded; for a concurrency-model workload those track the ramp it actually
performed, so plateau boundaries can be measured. Reading a native primitive k6 already publishes is
precisely what ADR-026 asks for — no custom tag, no custom metric.

`DERIVED_FROM_PLAN` is the honest answer for arrival-rate workloads. k6 does not emit the configured
target rate, so only the *anchor* is observed — the first sample the run produced — while the
boundaries themselves come from planned durations. Since most calibrated workloads are arrival-rate,
this is the common case, and saying so is the point.

*The consequence is not cosmetic.* A signal aligned by a derived boundary may still be reported as
correlated with a breakpoint, and never strengthens the finding. `ResourcePressure.strength` will not
return `HIGH` on a derived alignment however well the timestamps line up: those timestamps are Vortex's
own arithmetic, not independent corroboration, and letting them raise confidence would manufacture
certainty rather than establish it.

The cumulative walk itself now lives in one place, `StageWindows`. It previously existed in both the
analyzer and the k6 aggregator, and two implementations of one rule is one more than the rule
survives — they would eventually disagree about which stage a sample belonged to, and the
disagreement would surface as a bottleneck attributed to the wrong level of load.

=== Absence carries a cause

`TelemetryAvailability` distinguishes `NO_DATA`, `UNSUPPORTED`, `UNREACHABLE`, `UNAUTHORIZED` and
`MALFORMED`. `ObservabilityProvider.collect` returns observations *and* gaps together, so a provider
that answered for eleven metrics and failed on the twelfth reports eleven measurements and one
explained gap rather than forcing the caller to guess which happened.

The reason survives ingestion into `ObservabilityEvidence.gaps`, and each gap carries a remedy. A
provider that was asked and could not answer stays in `providersConsulted` with its reason: omitting
it would leave the reader thinking nobody had looked there.

Nothing here weakens the existing rule. A genuine absence still never becomes a numeric zero.

=== Correlation markers are enrichment, never a precondition

Dynatrace can be told a run is happening, which marks it on Dynatrace's own timeline. Reading metrics
and ingesting events need *different* permissions, and a production tenant may grant the first and
refuse the second.

So marker creation is attempted and its failure changes nothing about the telemetry: the provider
degrades to `CorrelationCapability.QUERY_ONLY`, records one gap saying the run is still findable by
its window, and goes on collecting. A measurement is never downgraded to missing because a marker
could not be written.

`QUERY_ONLY` is also Prometheus's permanent answer, which is a fact about Prometheus rather than a
shortfall in the integration.

== Consequences

*Gained.* A reader can tell a measured coincidence from a computed one, and the difference is enforced
in the strength calculation rather than left to the reader's judgement. An engineer who notices a
missing signal is told which of five quite different things went wrong, and what to do about it.
Telemetry survives a permission Vortex was never granted. The stage walk has one home.

*Cost.* Two more things to render and to explain, and evidence that frequently has to say "derived"
where a reader wanted "measured" — most calibrated workloads are arrival-rate, so most stage
alignments carry the weaker label. `MeasuredResults` grew two fields and `StageObservation` two more.
A reader skimming the Dynatrace adapter will find marker handling that looks like dead weight until
they hit a tenant that refuses it.

*Trade accepted.* Vortex could have aligned stages from the plan, said nothing about how, and produced
findings that read more confidently. Every one of those findings would have been a claim about
timestamps Vortex generated itself, presented as though the service had corroborated it. A weaker
statement that is true is worth more than a stronger one that is only usually true — particularly in
the part of a report most likely to be quoted out of context.
